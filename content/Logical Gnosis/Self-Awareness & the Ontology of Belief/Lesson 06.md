___
## How Does a Worldview Acquire the Feeling of Objective Reality?

___
## Lesson 6

We ended with something worth holding carefully.

A framework can remain fully active

while disappearing from view.

Not weakening.

Not resolving.

Simply becoming invisible,

while its effects continue exactly as before.

Ask yourself something first.

**Does a belief usually stay alone?**

Think of the missed call again.

Maybe your friend is angry with you.

Notice what tends to arrive right behind that thought,

almost without a gap.

Perhaps:

_People eventually get tired of me._

Or:

_I always expect the worst._

Or:

_Relationships require constant effort, or they fall apart._

Ask yourself:

**Where did that second thought come from?**

Did you go looking for it?

Or did it simply arrive,

already fitting,

already confirming the first?

Notice what just happened.

One stabilized configuration did not merely organize an event.

It became connected with another stabilized configuration,

already available,

and the two began to reinforce one another.

Now look at the mechanism more carefully.

The same three Functions are still operating.

But now something has changed.

They are no longer operating around one isolated configuration.

They are operating within a growing network.

Several previously formed configurations,

already connected through repeated experience,

can re-enter the new manifestation together.

The missed call is therefore not simply:

_phone call unanswered._

It can become:

_distance._

_rejection._

_disappointment._

_evidence._

The event is differentiated within an already organized field.

And from that field,

new configurations can emerge.

Perhaps:

_This confirms that people eventually leave._

Or:

_I should withdraw before I am rejected._

Or:

_I should ask what happened instead of assuming._

The important point is not which configuration forms.

The important point is that

**something new can be formed from the possibilities available within the present interaction.**

Ask yourself what happens when these three Functions repeatedly converge around the same configurations.

The same configuration becomes easier to activate.

More rapidly available.

More strongly connected.

More capable of recruiting other configurations.

And now something begins to emerge.

A belief about yourself connects to a belief about other people.

A belief about other people connects to a belief about relationships.

A belief about relationships connects to configurations concerning what may happen.

Those configurations influence attention.

Attention shapes what becomes noticeable.

What becomes noticeable supplies further material for interpretation.

Interpretation can then reinforce the original configuration.

The circle closes.

Ask yourself:

**Is this still a collection of separate beliefs?**

Or has something else begun to exist—

something none of the individual beliefs is by itself?

Give it its ordinary name.

**A worldview.**

**But what exactly is a worldview?**

Is it a list you could recite if someone asked what you believe?

Not necessarily.

It is better understood as a field.

Something you move through,

rather than something you consult.

Something that organizes experience continuously,

rather than something you occasionally retrieve as information.

Now do something simple.

Look around the room you are in right now.

Notice the walls.

The light.

The nearest objects.

The sounds around you.

Ask yourself:

**Does any of it look interpreted?**

Be honest.

It does not.

It looks like the world.

Plain.

Given.

Simply there.

Ask yourself why that should be strange.

We have spent the previous lessons watching belief operate—

through the three Functions,

continuously,

beneath attention.

That organization has not stopped.

It is operating in this room,

right now,

as you read these words.

So ask yourself:

**Why does the room not feel organized?**

Why does it feel found,

rather than interpreted?

Think of a perfectly clean glass window.

When the glass is perfectly clean,

you do not see the glass.

You see the street outside.

The trees.

The sky.

The people moving beyond it.

The glass is still there.

It is still part of the condition through which you see.

But it has disappeared from the field of attention.

When does the glass become visible?

A smudge.

A crack.

A reflection.

Something interrupts its transparency.

A network of beliefs can function in a similar way.

The events of your life are the street.

The worldview is the glass.

When the organization operates seamlessly,

the framework itself disappears.

You do not experience the interpreting.

You experience what has been interpreted.

Ask yourself:

**What would a worldview have to become in order for this to happen?**

It would have to become sufficiently integrated

that its individual operations no longer announce themselves.

Sufficiently stable

that its distinctions feel natural.

Sufficiently familiar

that its configurations can be reintroduced immediately.

It would have to organize enough of experience

that experience itself begins to arrive already structured through it.

A worldview does not usually announce:

_I am now interpreting this situation according to my worldview._

It simply operates.

And when it operates continuously enough,

consistently enough,

and without being noticed,

its effects can become indistinguishable,

in experience,

from reality itself.

The interpretation disappears from view.

What remains

is the feeling of obviousness.

The framework is still operating.

But it no longer appears

as a framework.

Only what it organizes appears.

Now look at the room you are in again.

Do not ask what is physically present.

Instead,

notice what becomes relevant to you.

The door.

The light.

The position of an object.

The possibility of movement.

A memory associated with the place.

Now ask yourself:

**Would someone with a different history**

**notice exactly the same things?**

Perhaps the physical arrangement

would remain the same.

But what becomes relevant

might be very different.

A different history,

different purposes,

and different learned associations

can organize the same configuration

of things in different ways.

What changes?

Not necessarily the room.

The organization of experience.

And yet,

from within your own experience,

that organization does not usually appear

as an organization.

It simply appears

as what is there.

Now ask yourself:

**How obvious does something feel?**

And:

**How true is it?**

Are these the same question?

No.

One concerns

how experience is organized.

The other concerns

what is actually the case.

A belief can feel completely natural

and still be mistaken.

A conviction can feel self-evident

and still depend upon a particular history.

More precisely,

a belief always depends upon

a particular history.

A distinction can feel inherent in reality

while being perceived differently

through different structures

through which reality

is encountered.

Ask yourself:

**Could something feel obvious**

**simply because its organization**

**has become familiar enough**

**to disappear from view?**

Think of one thing

you regard as simply obvious.

Something you would never ordinarily

think to question.

Now ask:

**Could this feel equally obvious**

**to someone whose previously formed configurations**

**were entirely different from yours?**

Do not rush to answer.

Just notice what the question does.

We have not discovered

that your beliefs are false.

We have discovered something more basic.

**Obviousness and truth are not the same phenomenon.**

One tells us something

about how a world is experienced.

The other would have to tell us something

about what is actually the case.

And now notice what happens

when this process extends

across an entire network.

The separate beliefs

stop being visible as beliefs.

Their relations

stop being visible as relations.

The organizing framework

stops appearing as a framework.

What remains?

A world.

Not _my worldview._

Not _my interpretation._

Simply:

_the way things are._

Ask yourself:

**What has disappeared from view?**

The world?

Or the structure

through which the world

has become intelligible?

Hold that distinction.

Because if an entire architecture

can disappear this completely

while remaining fully active,

we have reached a new problem.

The question is no longer simply:

**What do I believe?**

It is no longer even:

**How do my beliefs organize experience?**

The deeper question is:

**What, exactly, is operating**

**inside an organization**

**that has become invisible to itself?**

That is where we go next.
