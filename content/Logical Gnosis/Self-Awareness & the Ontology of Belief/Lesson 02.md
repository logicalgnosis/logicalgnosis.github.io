## How Does Manifestation Become Organized for an Observer?

___
## Lesson 2

We ended the last lesson with a question.

**How does Manifestation become organized for an observer?**

More precisely:

**If the Witnessing Pole is the pole to which Manifestation is present, what organizes Manifestation as it becomes present?**

Let's not answer it yet.

Let's look.

Notice the room around you again.

Not as a list of objects.

As it actually appears.

Some things stand out immediately.

The page in front of you.

A sound that just occurred.

Perhaps your own hands.

Other things remain less prominent.

The texture of the wall.

The exact pressure of your feet against the floor.

The distant sounds outside the room.

You did not deliberately construct all of these distinctions.

And yet,

the field is not simply one undivided mass.

Something is already differentiated.

So ask yourself:

**What makes one thing stand out from another?**

Look at the page.

Look at the desk beneath it.

You do not experience them as one continuous object.

There is a boundary.

The page is this.

The desk is that.

Your hand is here.

The air around it is there.

You do not first calculate these differences

and then experience them.

The differences are already present

in the way the field appears.

Now try something.

Look at the room

without deliberately naming anything.

Do not say "wall."

Do not say "table."

Do not say "hand."

Simply look.

The names can disappear.

But does the differentiation disappear with them?

Apparently not.

Even without names,

one region remains distinct from another.

One shape from another.

One sound from another.

One sensation from another.

So the distinction is not simply a product of verbal labeling.

Something more basic is occurring.

The field is being differentiated.

For now,

let us call this the **Differentiation Function**.

Hold that.

Now notice something else.

Look at the page again.

You have already read the previous sentence.

You know what this sentence is referring to.

You do not need to reconstruct the entire previous paragraph from nothing.

Something from what has already happened is available now.

Ask yourself:

**Where did that come from?**

The previous words are no longer physically present in exactly the same way.

And yet their significance can become available again.

A word you learned yesterday can appear in your thought today.

A place you visited years ago can become present again as a memory.

A previous experience can become relevant to what is happening now.

Something was already formed.

Now that previously formed configuration becomes available again.

It enters the present manifestation.

Try it directly.

Think of a familiar place.

Perhaps a room from your childhood.

You are not creating that place for the first time.

You are bringing something previously experienced into the present field.

A configuration that was formed before

is becoming present again.

Notice the difference.

The earlier experience is not occurring now in its original situation.

Yet something of its configuration

has become available within the present.

For now,

let us call this the **Recall Function**.

Recall does not create the original configuration again from nothing.

It brings something already formed

back into the present.

And when previously formed configurations become available again,

continuity becomes possible.

The present does not have to begin from zero.

What has already been formed

can participate in what is happening now.

Hold that.

Now look at what happens when these two Functions meet.

A familiar object appears.

Perhaps you recognize it immediately.

Recognition is not simply the object standing there.

Something from previous experience becomes available,

and the present content is encountered within that relation.

The present is differentiated.

The previously formed configuration is recalled.

And the two participate in a new moment of experience.

But something else can happen.

Something genuinely new can emerge.

Consider a simple example.

You encounter a problem.

You understand some of its elements.

You recognize some familiar relations.

You recall things you have learned before.

You distinguish the relevant features.

And yet,

none of those recalled configurations

is itself the solution.

Something new has to become actual.

Perhaps you suddenly see a relation

you had not seen before.

Perhaps a familiar idea takes on a new function.

Perhaps several previously separate elements form a configuration

that did not exist for you before.

Notice the difference.

The previous material has become available.

The relevant elements have become distinguishable and related.

And yet,

something new has appeared.

A new configuration has been realized.

Ask yourself:

**Was that new configuration already present as an object,

waiting somewhere to be noticed?**

Or did something genuinely new become actual?

Try to notice the difference.

A possibility is not yet an actual configuration.

Before the insight occurs,

several configurations may remain possible.

But Actualization is not simply the selection

of one possibility from a list.

It is the emergence of a new configuration

through the reorganization of what has already been formed

under new conditions.

Perhaps an old relation is reorganized.

Perhaps familiar elements acquire a new relation.

Perhaps several previously separate configurations

come together in a way that was not previously established.

The resulting configuration can therefore contain

a new relation,

a new function,

or a new organizational capacity.

And once it emerges,

the situation is no longer exactly what it was before.

The new configuration changes what can happen next.

New relations become available.

New possibilities appear.

Some previous possibilities are no longer available in the same way.

This is not simply movement toward the future.

It is the emergence of a new organization

from what was already available

under a new set of conditions.

For now,

let us call this the **Actualization Function**.

Now stop.

Look at the three Functions together.

Something previously formed can become present again.

**Recall Function.**

What is present can become distinguishable,

structured,

and related.

**Differentiation Function.**

A new configuration can emerge

through the reorganization of what has already been formed

under new conditions.

**Actualization Function.**

These Functions do not have to operate one after another.

They can operate together.

Now ask a further question.

**What happens to a configuration once it has become established?**

It does not simply disappear without a trace.

Its organization can become available again through Recall,

influencing new organizations and configurations of experience.

So the process is not a straight line.

It is not simply:

past → present → future.

It is a continual transformation,

in which all three Functions participate together

in the organization of each manifestation.

What has been actualized

becomes part of what has been formed.

What has been formed

can later become available again.

What is recalled

enters a new present.

What is present

is differentiated within that manifestation.

And from that configuration,

new possibilities can become available for Actualization.

Pause here.

Try to see this without turning it into a theory.

Think of something you learned recently.

Something from previous experience became available.

The elements of the present situation became distinguishable.

Perhaps something changed.

A new understanding appeared.

A relation became visible.

A new way of organizing what you already knew became possible.

Something has entered the continuity of experience.

Something has changed the conditions of what can happen next.

Now return to the original question.

**If Manifestation is present to a Witnessing Pole, where do these Functions belong?**

Are they simply objects that appear to the Witnessing Pole?

No.

Recall is not a remembered object standing somewhere behind experience.

Differentiation is not another object that separates objects.

Actualization is not an object called "newness."

These are Functions through which Manifestation becomes organized.

They are not additional pieces of content.

And yet,

they are not detached from content either.

They become visible through what happens to content.

A configuration becomes present again.

Something becomes distinguishable.

Something becomes localized.

Relations become structured.

A new configuration emerges.

The Functions reveal themselves through transformation.

So perhaps the better question is not:

**What object performs these Functions?**

But:

**What Functions are operating through the formation of experience?** 

Now return once more to the room.

The page.

The sounds.

Your body.

The thoughts appearing as you read.

Do not try to explain them.

Just observe.

Something from before can become present again.

Something present can become distinct

from something else.

Something new can become actual.

Can you notice all three?

Not as three objects.

Not as three stages.

But as three different Functions

within the ongoing formation of experience.

For now,

hold only this:

**Recall makes the previously formed available again.**

**Differentiation structures what is present.**

**Actualization increases the complexity of experiential organization.**

And notice what follows.

What has been formed

can enter what comes next.

What is present

can be reorganized through differentiation.

What becomes actual

can change the field of future possibility.

Each new manifestation therefore inherits conditions

from what has already been formed,

while also transforming the conditions

from which further manifestation can arise.

This is why the three Functions matter.

This is where we will go next.

We will examine

what happens when one of these Functions

is no longer available.

That question allows us

to see more precisely

what each Function contributes

to the organization of Manifestation.
